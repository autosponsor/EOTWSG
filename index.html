A calm place to begin

Make a plan you can explain, practice, and update.

EOTWSG is a general educational guide for household preparedness. It does not replace local emergency instructions, medical guidance, or professional assistance.

Read the first stepsUse public resources

If it is urgent

If someone is in immediate danger, contact local emergency services. For current alerts and instructions, follow your local authorities.

Keep the first pass simple

Three questions worth answering first

01

How will we receive alerts?

Identify local alert channels and decide how your household will share time-sensitive information.

02

Where will we reconnect?

Agree on familiar meeting points and a communication plan before disruptions make coordination harder.

03

What changes for our household?

Consider medications, accessibility needs, children, older adults, pets, language, work, and regular travel.

The first hour of planning

Build a baseline, then adapt it.

    Write down the household plan
